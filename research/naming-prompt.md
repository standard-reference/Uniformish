# Naming + trademark prompt — for a claude.ai chat session

Companion to `market-research-prompt.md`. Use this one when the brand name needs to
be settled — e.g. before buying a domain or ordering woven labels.

**Why this is higher-stakes than a normal naming exercise:** the brand's
differentiator is a *sewn-in woven label*. A name change after labels are ordered
means scrapping physical stock, not just redirecting a domain. Treat the name as
expensive to reverse.

**What this pass cannot do:** clear a trademark. A chat session with web search can
find obvious conflicts and rule candidates out, which is genuinely useful — it cannot
tell you a name is safe. Anything that survives this needs a real clearance search
before money is spent on domains, labels, or packaging.

**Return path:** the shortlist and the conflict findings go into issue #5 and, once
chosen, into `CLAUDE.md`. Don't let it live only in a chat thread.

---

## The prompt

> I need to settle the name for a streetwear brand, and I need you to be
> disqualifying rather than generative — I'd rather leave with two viable names than
> twenty pretty ones. Use web search and cite what you find.
>
> **Constraints that matter:**
>
> - Category is apparel — **Nice Class 25** is the trademark class that counts, plus
>   Class 35 if I ever do retail services.
> - The name gets **sewn into garments on a woven label**. Changing it later means
>   scrapping physical stock, so I need to weight conflict risk heavily over how
>   nice a name sounds.
> - I need a **matching domain** — I'm setting up email on a custom domain, so
>   `.com` strongly preferred, and I need to know what's actually available.
> - Social handles matter but are secondary to the trademark and domain.
> - It's a global-ish DTC brand, so US (USPTO) and UK/EU (IPO / EUIPO) all matter.
>
> **Where I am now.** The working name is **"Unit"**. I also considered **"Tones"**.
> My understanding is that both have existing conflicts in apparel — I want that
> confirmed or corrected before anything else, because if "Unit" is actually
> workable that's the cheapest outcome by far.
>
> **What the brand is, so the name has something to fit.** Accessible premium
> streetwear built on a colour system rather than a palette. The rule is "one colour,
> head to toe" — every piece designed to be worn as a full monotone fit, one hue from
> shirt to socks. Pieces may step lighter as they go down the body, and the exact
> lightness step is published as a spec value. The identity is engineered and
> systematic — spec sheets, codes, measured tolerances — rather than loud or
> logo-driven. Positioned above Uniqlo U, below Fear of God Essentials.
>
> **Do this, in order:**
>
> 1. **Kill or confirm "Unit" first.** Search USPTO, UK IPO and EUIPO for live marks
>    in Class 25. Tell me specifically what exists, who owns it, and whether it's
>    live or dead. Same for "Tones". If either is workable, say so plainly — I'd
>    rather keep the name I have.
> 2. **If both are dead, propose 8–12 candidates** that fit the brand above. I'm
>    drawn to short, plain, systematic words — the kind of name that sounds like it
>    belongs on a spec sheet rather than a mood board. Avoid anything that needs
>    explaining, anything with an invented spelling, and anything that reads as a
>    tech startup.
> 3. **Screen every candidate before you show it to me.** For each: Class 25 conflicts
>    (with what you actually found, not a guess), `.com` availability, and whether
>    something well-known already owns the word in a way that makes the name
>    unusable in practice even if legally clear.
> 4. **Rank by risk, not by taste.** Put the ones most likely to survive clearance at
>    the top, and say what would kill each one.
>
> **How to answer.** Mark each claim ✅ (found it in a register or a WHOIS/registrar
> result), 🟡 (secondary source or inference), ❓ (couldn't determine). Where you
> can't check a register directly, say so rather than guessing — a confident wrong
> answer here costs real money.
>
> Finish with: **the two or three names you'd actually put money behind**, and for
> each, exactly what a lawyer would need to clear before I order labels.
