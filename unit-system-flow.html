01

Supply

Three vendors, each doing the one thing they're actually good at — not one factory for everything.

PRIMARY · GARMENTS
Apliiq

Cut-and-sew tee, crewneck, pant, shorts. Woven labels — not printed — blanks up to 490 GSM, in-house production in LA and Philadelphia. The private-label depth the spec-sheet identity needs; a printed tag doesn't sell "engineered," a woven one does.

SUBLIMATION · PATTERNS + SLIP-ON
Printful

Full-canvas sublimation for anything that needs an exact hex rather than a stock dye lot: the Bridge/Novelty patterns, the solid socks, and the Slip-On canvas shoe. Sublimation is also the only route to a specified hex rather than a stock dye lot. Zero MOQ.

BACKUP · NOT IN PRODUCTION
Tapstitch

Held in reserve — if Apliiq's colorway catalog can't clear the match spec, or lead time becomes the bottleneck. Purpose-built for the $40–80 tier, 250–400 GSM defaults, faster turnaround, less private-label depth.

SOLID colors are matched to existing dye names, not custom-mixed — the one place in this flow where the specced hex and the shipped hex can differ. Check any new colorway against the actual catalog before it ships.

The same applies to STEP, and harder. A computed hex cannot be ordered from a POD supplier on dyed blanks — Apliiq ships stock dye lots. Every STEP below is a target; it only becomes a product once it is snapped to a real colorway in the supplier’s range and the snapped result is re-checked against the match spec. Where nothing in range lands outside the forbidden band, that STEP does not exist. Specified hexes are achievable only via sublimation — which is why the patterns, socks and Slip-On sit with Printful.

02

Palette

Six hues, two grades, one match spec computed in OKLCh.

THE RULE

One hue, head to toe — not one hex. Stay in a hue family and the whole fit reads as monotone, whether every piece is an exact match or steps lighter toward the feet.

GRADE · SOLID

Every piece the exact same colorway, hex-for-hex. The tightest version of the rule — badge it SOLID, no spec value needed.

GRADE · STEP

Same hue family, one deliberate lightness break at the waist — pieces get lighter moving down the body. Badge it STEP and show the ΔL, same as any other spec value.

GRADE · MULTI-STEP — IN DEVELOPMENT, NOT LIVE

Same mechanism as STEP, extended to 3–4 pieces at distinct lightness levels instead of one break. Not a soft gradient — each adjacent pair still needs its own ≥0.13L jump to individually clear the forbidden band above.

Direction is computed per color from real gamut headroom, not fixed to "lighter down the body" — and both directions stay available wherever the math clears the floor, not just whichever wins:

The mechanism isn't a complementary-wheel pairing — that tends to look costumey once it's on a body instead of a color wheel. Every color here shares the same narrow band of value and chroma, which is what makes a STEP read as a decision instead of a mismatch. Crossing a row instead of holding a column works the same way — matched for value and undertone, so it never looks accidental.

Each STEP hex below holds hue and chroma from the SOLID anchor and moves lightness by ΔL 0.15 — the middle of the allowed range — then gamut-maps back into sRGB. Bone is the one color that can't clear the floor: pushed 0.13L lighter it runs into white before the step is deliberate enough to read as one, so Bone ships SOLID only.

05

Assembly

How a grade becomes a purchase — no single piece ships alone.

THE FLOOR

Minimum two pieces, same colorway, every order. No single-item checkout — "we don't sell orphan pieces" is the stated rule, not a workaround.

ENFORCEMENT STOPS AT CHECKOUT

The floor governs what ships, not what's worn. Nothing polices the pair after the sale, and nothing should — a lifestyle obligation stacked on an unearned trust ask costs more buyers than it protects. The identity holds in the object itself (woven label, grade, code), worn together or not.

KIT

3–4 pieces, sold as one bundled SKU — the full monotone story, one add-to-cart. The only purchase path Aftercare (Stage 06) applies to.

DUO · BOTTOM ANCHOR

Tee + pant/short, listed individually and grouped at cart — not a bundled SKU. Full monotone, replaces what's already in the closet. Live now.

DUO · SLIP-ON ANCHOR

Tee + slip-on, listed individually and grouped at cart — not a bundled SKU. Interleaves with any pants already owned. Live now, via Printful.

06

Aftercare

The floor is enforced at checkout; this is the release valve — and it only applies to one of the two purchase paths.

RETURNS — KITS ONLY

Applies to Kits only. Duos are grouped from individually final-sale pieces, not a bundle — there's nothing to partially unwind, so no return path exists for them by construction. For a Kit: box up whatever wasn't kept, ship it back, full refund — buyer covers return shipping. One rule holds on the way out too — a return can size a Kit down, but can't drop it below the same two-piece, same-colorway floor everything else has to clear. Handled personally, not routed through Apliiq or Printful; neither covers buyer's-remorse returns, so this is a cost carried on purpose, not a platform feature. Priced into Kit margin. Cash refund on unworn/tags-on; store credit is the fallback for anything softer.

Roadmap

Where each supplier and silhouette actually stands right now.

PHASE 0 — ACTIVE
Self-built

Zero-MOQ via Apliiq, sourced blanks in-range. Every SKU proven before real capital moves. STEP grade takes buildable four-piece fits from 5 (strict hex) to 14 — but that figure was computed against AS Colour's catalog and its 3-colors-per-style shorts cap, and does not transfer to the palette above unaltered. Recompute once the blank house is settled. Launch with 5–6 fits, the rest staggered in as drops. Slip-On ships from day one too, via Printful.

PHASE 1 — PENDING
Capability outsource

Unverified: no Printful slip-on has been confirmed to exist in a suitable form — check before the Stage 04 listing is treated as launch-ready. Triggered by construction, not revenue — the first build we can't run zero-MOQ. A more constructed slip-on (real leather/suede, proper last) is the leading candidate whenever it earns the MOQ; the canvas version stays in the line either way.

PHASE 2 — PENDING
Vendor network

A small bench of specialists matched to what each does best, not one factory for everything.

BLANK HOUSE — SPLIT UNDER TEST, PALETTE NOT FINAL

The palette above is sourced as Comfort Colors. Apliiq's entire Comfort Colors range is 13 products and every one is a top — there are no Comfort Colors bottoms on Apliiq. So the six-color core four is not buildable as written, and a split is forced rather than optional.

The candidate split keeps everything on Apliiq, so the woven label survives: Comfort Colors garment-dyed tops + Independent Trading Co pigment-dyed bottoms — PRM50PTPD joggers and PRM50STPD shorts, a matched pant and short in one family. Both houses use the same pigment/garment dye process, so they coordinate on hand and finish, not just on hex. That matched pant/short pair is a structural advantage over AS Colour, where no shorts style carries more than three colors and each color implies a different silhouette.

What decides it: whether Comfort Colors tops and ITC pigment-dyed bottoms actually share colorways. Not yet checkable — ITC rate-limits and the wholesale distributors block automated access, so this needs get_product against Apliiq once API credentials exist. Fallback if the overlap is thin: keep these color names as brand language and re-point them at AS Colour hexes.

Until that resolves, the STEP hexes in Stage 02 are targets, not products.
